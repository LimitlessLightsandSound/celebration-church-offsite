The actual design problem

Faces in front of the wall, not silhouettes against it

A sixteen-foot p2.6 wall at working brightness will swallow anyone standing in front of it. That is the real problem on this build, and it is why the rig is weighted the way it is. Eight Prolights ECL ProfileFS on 19° lens tubes put hard-edged front light on the platform, tight enough to keep spill off the panels so the wall does not wash out. Six Martin MAC Aura XB sit behind and above for hair and shoulder separation, which is what actually lifts a person off a bright screen. The twelve Robe CycFX8 then do colour on the room rather than on faces, so the palette can move through the service without changing how anyone looks on the platform.

Why eight crew build it and two crew run it

The work happens on Friday

Friday is where the labour is. Eight people build the wall and stage, run power and data, hang and focus the rig, map the panels, tune the PA and build the GrandMA2 show file. By 5:00 PM the system is not just assembled, it is programmed. That is what collapses Sunday down to two people: an A1 mixing and an L1 running cues off a file that already exists. Nobody is troubleshooting a data run or writing looks during the service, because that work was finished two days earlier.

power chain

Generator to fixture, in four steps

Everything on this job runs off our own generator rather than assuming a service we have not metered. From there it is camlock into an Indu Cube 120/208V distro, Socapex out on two 100' and two 50' runs, then Edison and True1 to each position. The video wall sits on its own leg of that distribution so a lighting fault cannot take the screen with it.

If the room turns out to have real service we can tie into safely, we take the generator off and reduce the number rather than quietly keeping it in. Same with rigging: the wall and PA are quoted ground-supported, but if there are good points overhead and you would rather fly part of it, we will price that once we have walked the space.
